---
title: Secure your sign-in
description: Add an authenticator app code to your password, keep backup codes, sign in with a passkey, and know what to do if you lose access.
---

Your sign-in protects every workspace you belong to, including approvals and changes to connected systems. Open **Settings → Security** to add a code step after your password, or a passkey that replaces the password. These settings belong to your account and apply in every workspace.

## Change your password

Under **Settings → Security → Password**, select **Change password**. Enter your current password and a new password with at least 12 characters. Select **Sign out other devices** to keep only this device signed in.

With two-factor authentication on, you need to have signed in within the last 15 minutes. Otherwise, sign out and sign in again with your second factor. Luma emails you after a password change when email delivery is configured.

## Review active sessions

**Active sessions** shows your signed-in browsers and devices, IP address and last activity. **This session** marks your current sign-in. Activity updates at most every five minutes. Device descriptions are estimates; an IP address does not identify an exact location.

Select **Sign out** for one session or **Sign out all other sessions**, then confirm. Ended sessions are rejected on their next request. Signing out this session returns you to the sign-in page.

## Turn on two-factor authentication

With two-factor authentication on, Luma asks for a 6-digit code from an authenticator app after your password. Any app that supports time-based codes works, such as 1Password, Google Authenticator or Microsoft Authenticator.

1. Under **Authenticator app**, select **Set up** and enter your password.
2. **Save your backup codes** shows 10 codes. Store them somewhere safe, such as your password manager; **Copy codes** copies them all. You won’t see them again. Then select **I’ve saved my codes**.
3. Scan the QR code with your authenticator app, or enter the **Setup key**.
4. Enter the 6-digit code the app shows and select **Turn on**.

Two-factor authentication starts only once the code is confirmed. If you close the dialog earlier, nothing changes and signing in still needs only your password.

If a code is rejected, check that your device sets its time automatically. Codes change every 30 seconds.

## Sign in with a code

After your email and password, Luma asks you to **Confirm it’s you**. Enter the current code from your authenticator app and select **Verify**. If you don’t have your device, select **Use a backup code**. Each backup code works once.

Enter the code within 10 minutes of entering your password. If the sign-in attempt expires, or after five incorrect codes, select **Sign in again** and enter your password again. After repeated incorrect codes, Luma locks code entry for 15 minutes.

## Replace your backup codes

Select **New backup codes**, enter your password and select **Create codes**. Your earlier codes stop working immediately. Do this if you’ve used most of your codes or think someone else has seen them.

## Use a passkey

A passkey signs you in with your fingerprint, face, device PIN or a security key. It can’t be phished or reused on another site. Signing in with a passkey skips both your password and the code step.

1. Under **Passkeys**, optionally enter a name, such as “Work laptop”.
2. Select **Add passkey** and follow your device’s prompt.

On the sign-in page, select **Sign in with a passkey**. Add a passkey on each device you sign in from, unless your password manager syncs passkeys between devices. The list shows whether each one is a **Synced passkey** or a **Passkey on one device**.

To add a passkey, you need to have signed in within the last day. If Luma asks you to sign out and sign in again first, do that and then add the passkey. If your browser can’t create passkeys, use a current version of Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari.

## Remove a passkey or turn off two-factor authentication

Select **Remove** next to a passkey, then **Remove passkey**. Also delete it from your device or password manager.

To stop using codes, select **Turn off** under **Authenticator app** and enter your password. Your backup codes stop working. Passkeys you’ve added keep working.

## Check recent security activity

**Recent security activity** lists your sign-ins, failed sign-in attempts and changes to how you sign in, newest first. This includes turning two-factor authentication on or off, replacing backup codes, adding or removing passkeys, being signed out, including by Luma support, and Luma support disabling or enabling your account or scheduling or cancelling its deletion. Each entry shows when it happened, the approximate device and the IP address. **This session** marks your current sign-in. Select **Show older activity** to go further back.

Luma keeps devices and IP addresses for 90 days and the activity for one year. Only you see your activity; Luma support can review it only while you have approved a request, or during emergency access.

If you see a sign-in you don’t recognize, remove passkeys you don’t recognize, replace your backup codes and turn on two-factor authentication if it’s off. Then sign out all other sessions under **Active sessions** and change your password.

## Answer Luma support requests

Luma support can't look at your account details without your approval. When a support team member asks, you get an email, and the request appears in **Settings › Security** with their reason.

- **Approve** gives that team member 30 minutes of read-only access from now. They see your workspaces and roles, whether two-factor authentication is on, how many passkeys you have, your active sessions and your security activity. They never see your password, codes or passkeys, and they can't open your workspaces this way.
- **Decline** refuses the request. A request you don't answer lapses after 24 hours.
- While approved access is open, **End inspection** ends it now.

For a security incident or legal request, Luma support can open 30 minutes of access without your approval. You get an email, it appears in your security activity, and it can't be revoked. Every request, answer and revocation appears in **Recent security activity**.

## If you lose access

- **Lost your authenticator device**: sign in with a backup code or a passkey. Then turn two-factor authentication off and set it up again on your new device.
- **Used up your backup codes**: sign in with your authenticator app or a passkey and select **New backup codes**.
- **Forgot your password**: select **Forgot password?** on the sign-in page and enter your email. The emailed link expires in 15 minutes and works once. Resetting signs out every session. Your two-factor authentication stays on. If email delivery is unavailable, contact Luma support.
- **“This account is disabled”**: Luma support has disabled your account, for example after a report that it was compromised. You can’t sign in with a password or a passkey, and none of your workspaces open. Contact your workspace owner or Luma support. Once support enables the account, you can sign in again and your workspaces and roles are as they were.

Without your authenticator app, a backup code or a passkey, you can’t finish signing in. Keep at least two of them available.

## Delete your account

To delete your Luma account, ask Luma support. If you own a workspace, make someone else an owner first: a workspace can’t lose its only owner.

- Luma support schedules the deletion and you get an email. From then on you can’t sign in, and none of your workspaces open. Workflows that run with your access pause until someone else takes them over.
- For 30 days, Luma support can cancel the deletion. Your account, workspaces and roles are then as they were before, and you get an email.
- After 30 days, your account is erased: your name, email, password, passkeys, two-factor settings, sessions, security activity and personal settings are deleted. Workspaces keep their records of your work, such as Activity entries and approvals, but they show **Former member** instead of your name. You get an email when the erasure starts, and it can’t be undone.
- If you ask for erasure under the GDPR (Art. 17), Luma support can erase your account right away instead of after 30 days, once your request is verified.

Erased data stays in Luma’s backups until they expire, and is never restored from them.
