---
title: Resolve an issue
description: Review a proposed change, approve, reject or escalate it, and confirm that the fix worked.
---

When Luma can fix a problem through a connected system, it proposes a change, for example setting Shopify stock to the quantity in your ERP. Nothing changes in your systems until the change passes your workspace’s approval rules. Afterward, Luma checks the result with a fresh read.

Open the change with **Review change** at the top of the full issue page, **Review proposed change** in **Overview**, or the **Proposed change** section. Changes that wait for a person also appear in [Needs you](/docs/needs-you).

## Who can decide

- Approving and rejecting need **Approve or reject changes**. The approver must be someone other than the person who requested the change, shown under **Requested by**.
- Escalating and closing an issue need **Work on issues**.

Without these permissions you can still read the whole issue, and the review says **You can’t make a decision on this issue right now.**

## Review the proposed change

**Review the proposed change** shows:

- The change and its risk: **Low risk**, **Medium risk** or **High risk**.
- **Target system** and **Target record**: which connection and which record will change.
- **Proposed changes**: the fields and the values Luma will set.
- **Expected outcome**: what Luma checks afterward to confirm the fix.
- **Evidence for this change**: the data the proposal relies on. Luma either keeps it current while you review, or shows until when it’s valid and when the proposal expires.

When the shared change review is available, compare **Before**, **After** and **Expected before dispatch**. The expected value is the baseline Luma will check again before sending the change. A changed baseline stops the action; review a fresh proposal before continuing.

The review identifies the provider record separately from the Luma record and lists the affected locations, orders and lines. **Unknown** means the proposal did not establish that value or the full affected count. It never means zero. A record created by the action has an unknown provider ID until the provider assigns one. Evidence shows its observation time and age; a recorded approval-rule result does not replace the current approval controls.

**A separate compensating action may be possible** means another reviewed action may correct the result. It is not an automatic undo. **Irreversible** changes, such as a sent message or supplier commitment, need particular care. When a proposal is replaced while you check it, expand **Previous proposal · replaced, cannot be approved** to compare the old review with the fresh one.

**Can’t be undone automatically** means the proposal has no way to reverse the change. Don’t assume you can undo it.

## Check what the approval rules require

A notice under the change says what your workspace’s approval rules require:

- **Approval needed**: someone who can approve changes must approve it before Luma sends it.
- **No approval needed**: Luma sends the change on its own and checks the outcome separately.
- **Not allowed by workspace rules**: Luma won’t send this change. Follow the recommended steps or escalate the issue.

If your workspace requires approval for every change, every change needs approval, even a low-risk one. The workspace also decides whether another person must approve. See [Approvals](/docs/workspace-settings#approvals). If you see **Approval rules changed**, refresh before you decide.

**Change unavailable** means the connection can’t make this change right now, for example because it lost access. **Proposal is out of date** means the investigation changed; wait for an updated proposal.

## Approve, reject or escalate

1. Read the change, its evidence and the approval notice.
2. Enter a **Reason for your decision (required)**, up to 1,000 characters. Luma saves it with your decision.
3. Choose **Approve change**, **Reject change** or **Escalate**.

Before it records your decision, Luma checks current conditions. Then it confirms what happened:

- **Approval recorded**: Luma recorded your approval. Sending the change and confirming the outcome are tracked separately.
- **Rejection recorded**: Luma won’t send the change. The issue stays open for follow-up.
- **Escalation recorded**: the issue is marked for further review. Escalating doesn’t change anything in your systems, and it doesn’t cancel a change that has already started.

If there’s no change to review, follow the recommended steps in **Overview**. To hand the issue to someone else, select **Escalate issue**, say why it needs escalation, and select **Confirm escalation**.

## Close an issue yourself

Close an issue when it needs no more work: for example, a false positive, a fix made outside Luma that Luma cannot verify, or a problem your team accepts.

1. Open the issue’s **Proposed change** section and select **Close issue**.
2. Enter a **Reason for closing (required)**, up to 1,000 characters.
3. If work may be in progress, confirm that it may continue, then select **Close issue**.

The issue leaves active Issues, Needs you and Home. It stays in History as **Closed manually**, with who closed it and why. Insights counts it separately from verified fixes.

Closing blocks pending proposals and new changes for this issue. It cannot cancel a change already sent or undo its effects. Running workflows may finish their current work and outcome checks, and uncertain changes can still be reconciled. If Luma cannot confirm workflow activity, it asks you to acknowledge this before closing.

New evidence of the same problem can open a new issue linked to the closed one. Repeated delivery of old evidence does not reopen it.

## When the proposal expires or changes

A proposal and its evidence are valid only for a limited time:

- **Approval window expired**: approving needs a new proposal based on fresh evidence. Refreshing the page doesn’t extend the deadline.
- **The proposed change was updated**: Luma found that the change needs to be different. Review the new details before you approve.
- **Checking current conditions**: Luma is reading the latest data before you continue. If it can’t, you see **Check before you decide**; select **Check again**.

Luma checks current conditions again right before it sends an approved change. If the change would be different, it asks for a new review instead of sending it.

## If your connection drops while you decide

If your network drops while you submit a decision, Luma may not know whether it was recorded. Before you do anything else:

1. Select **Check if it was recorded**. Luma looks up your decision.
2. If it wasn’t recorded, select **Send same decision again**. Luma sends the identical decision, so it can’t be recorded twice.

Retrying never turns a rejection into an approval. While you’re offline, decisions are paused until Luma can confirm the current state of the issue.

## Confirm the outcome

Approving a change doesn’t fix the issue. Follow the steps in **Change and outcome check**, or select **View change progress**:

1. **Waiting for approval** or **Change queued**: nothing has been sent yet.
2. **Sending change**: Luma is sending the change to the connected system.
3. **Change accepted**: the system accepted it. This doesn’t prove the issue is fixed.
4. **Checking outcome**: Luma reads the system again to see whether the expected outcome happened.

The issue then ends in one of these states:

- **Resolved and confirmed**: a fresh check confirmed the fix.
- **Outcome not confirmed**: the check didn’t find the expected result. Review the check’s evidence and the current findings.
- **Outcome unclear**: Luma couldn’t tell whether the change took effect. Check the record in the connected system before trying again.
- **Change failed**: the system didn’t make the change. Check the attempt details and current findings before you decide what to do next.

If you see **Closed manually**, someone closed the issue without a confirmed fix. That doesn’t confirm the problem was fixed.

If progress can’t load, you see **Progress couldn’t be confirmed**. Select **Refresh progress** before you rely on an earlier status.

To see how many issues were resolved and confirmed over time, open [Insights](/docs/insights).
