---
title: Install apps and connect your systems
description: Install the Shopify, JTL and Cin7 Core apps, connect your stores and ERP, and keep each connection working.
---

Apps connect Luma to the systems you already use. Each app, such as Shopify, JTL or Cin7 Core, adds its own connections, permissions and pages to your workspace. A connection is one store or ERP installation that Luma reads from and, where you allow it, changes. Open **Manage apps** from the sidebar, or press **G** then **C**.

## Find your apps

The **Manage apps** page has up to three lists:

- **Installed apps**: one row per app with what it connects, the names of its connections and one state for all of them, such as **Healthy**, **Importing** or **1 connection needs attention**. Choose the app to open its page. When a connection needs attention, the page opens at that connection.
- **Available apps**: apps you can install in this workspace.
- **Previously used apps**: apps that aren’t installed anymore but still have earlier connections. Open one to see them. Records those connections imported stay in Luma.

Everyone in the workspace can see apps and connections. Installing, uninstalling and approving app access needs **Manage apps**. Adding, validating and disconnecting connections needs **Manage connections**. Without these, the page says what your role can’t do.

## Install an app

1. Under **Available apps**, choose **Install** on the app.
2. Review what the app gets:
   - **Data and actions the app can use**, such as orders, stock or proposing actions. Items marked **Sensitive** can change data in your systems, move money, change access or approval rules, or read personal data.
   - **New permissions for roles**: the app’s own permissions. Shopify adds **Recover Shopify fulfillments** and **Correct Shopify inventory**. JTL adds **Send purchase orders to JTL** and **Change JTL shipping methods**. Owners and admins get them automatically. To give them to another role, edit the role in [Settings → Roles](/docs/roles-and-permissions).
3. Choose the install button in the dialog again. Luma opens the app’s page so you can add a connection.

An app works for the person using it and never does more than that person’s role allows. You can only grant access your own role includes. If yours doesn’t include all of it, the dialog says so and a workspace owner has to install the app.

## Approve new access after an update

When an app update asks for more access, its page says so and offers **Review new access**. The dialog lists the new data and actions the app asks for. Choose **Approve new access** to grant them. Until someone with **Manage apps** approves, the app keeps working with the access it already has.

## Connect Shopify

1. On the Shopify app’s page, choose **Add connection**.
2. Enter a **Connection name** your team will recognize, and the **Shop hostname**: the store’s `.myshopify.com` address, such as `your-store.myshopify.com`. Shopify lists it under **Settings → Domains**.
3. Choose **Continue to Shopify** and approve the access there. You can cancel before approving.

Shopify then returns you to Luma, which checks the access it received. **Connection added. Luma confirmed it can read the data it needs.** means you’re done. If Luma couldn’t confirm every permission, check the connection’s **Permissions**. If you declined in Shopify, no connection is added; add it again to retry.

Luma asks for access to read orders, products, inventory, returns and fulfillment, and to change inventory and fulfillment. Luma changes them only for the stock corrections and fulfillment recoveries people approve. Before you continue, the form also says:

- How much order history Luma can read. Shopify shares orders from the last 60 days unless access to older orders is approved for Luma and the store authorizes it.
- Whether customer details are available. They are imported only after Shopify approves access to customer data and the store grants it.
- Which access the store hasn’t granted. Reconnect the store to request it.

## Connect JTL Cloud

1. On the JTL app’s page, choose **Add connection** and set **Connection type** to **JTL Cloud**.
2. Enter a **Connection name**, the **JTL Cloud tenant ID** and an **Access token** from your JTL Cloud installation. Grant that installation access to orders, products, inventory and fulfillment.
3. Choose any [write access](#choose-what-luma-may-change-in-jtl) you want, then **Save connection**.
4. Choose **Validate access** on the new connection to see what JTL granted.

## Connect JTL-Wawi on-premise

1. Register Luma as an app in JTL-Wawi. JTL-Wawi gives you an access token and a challenge code.
2. On the JTL app’s page, choose **Add connection** and set **Connection type** to **JTL-Wawi on-premise**.
3. Enter:
   - **Connection name**.
   - **HTTPS API URL**: the address of your JTL-Wawi API. It must start with `https://` and end with `/`, for example `https://erp.example.com/api/eazybusiness/`.
   - **App ID** and **App version** of the app you registered.
   - **Access token** and **Challenge code** from JTL-Wawi.
4. Choose any write access, then **Save connection** and **Validate access**.

Luma only connects to on-premise addresses that your Luma administrator has allowed for your workspace. Send them the HTTPS API URL before you validate. Until the address is allowed, validation can’t succeed.

## Choose what Luma may change in JTL

A JTL connection always asks for read access to orders, products, inventory and fulfillment. When you add it, you can also allow:

- **Allow shipping method corrections**: needs read and write access to sales orders in JTL.
- **Allow sending approved purchase orders**: needs access to suppliers, purchase orders and items in JTL. Procurement uses it to [send purchase orders](/docs/procurement#send-an-order).
- **Import customers**: reads customers, including names, contact details and addresses, and matches them to their orders. Needs read access to customers in JTL. The customers then appear in [Customers](/docs/customers) with their order history.

These options can only be chosen when you add the connection. Luma uses customers and write actions only after it confirms that JTL granted them. People still need the matching app permission, and every change waits for approval.

## Connect Cin7 Core

1. In Cin7 Core, add an API application for Luma on the API setup page. Cin7 shows its account ID and application key.
2. On the Cin7 Core app’s page, choose **Add connection**.
3. Enter a **Connection name**, the **Account ID** and the **Application key**.
4. Choose **Import customers** if Luma should read customers, including names, contact details and addresses, and match them to their orders.
5. Choose **Save connection**, then **Validate access**.

Luma only reads from Cin7 Core and never changes anything there. Cin7 allows each API application 60 requests per minute, so the first import of a large account takes several hours. Give Luma its own API application: integrations that share one also share its limit.

## Check a connection

Each connection shows its state with what to do next, when Luma last read from it, whether it may only read or also change data, and whether changes are allowed. Below that, **Data** lists the last complete read of each type of record. Choose a type, or **View Data sync**, to see its sync.

| State                   | What it means                                                                                                                 | What to do                                                                                                                       |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Awaiting validation** | Luma hasn’t confirmed the credentials yet and can’t read anything.                                                            | Choose **Validate access**.                                                                                                      |
| **Importing**           | Luma confirmed access and is still importing. Some records may be missing until every type has arrived.                       | Nothing. **Data** shows which types arrived.                                                                                     |
| **Healthy**             | Every type of record is current.                                                                                              | Nothing.                                                                                                                         |
| **Delayed**             | Some data hasn’t been read for more than a day.                                                                               | Open **Data sync** to see which.                                                                                                 |
| **Read failing**        | Luma couldn’t confirm the credentials or every requested permission.                                                          | Check the access granted in the connected system, then **Validate access**, or open **Connection settings** to enter new access. |
| **Access lost**         | The connected system stopped accepting Luma’s access, for example because the token was revoked or the app was removed there. | Choose **Reconnect** and enter new access.                                                                                       |
| **Disconnected**        | Someone disconnected it. Luma no longer reads or changes anything through it. It’s listed below the live connections.         | Choose **Reconnect** to set it up again.                                                                                         |

**Permissions** lists what Luma may read and change through the connection. Permissions Luma doesn’t use yet are listed under **Not in use** with the reason: before validation, or because the connected system hasn’t confirmed them. When a connection needs attention, this list opens by itself. **Details** shows the account or address the connection points to, when it was added and its ID for support. **History** lists the latest changes to the connection, such as who added, checked, renamed or reconnected it, and links to all of them in [Activity](/docs/activity). It's shown to people who can see restricted activity.

To check every connection of an app at once, choose **Validate all** above the connections. Luma checks them one by one and says how many it confirmed; each connection then shows its own state.

If a form says Luma couldn’t confirm whether your change was saved, close it and choose **Refresh list** before you try again.

## Rename a connection

Open the connection's actions menu (**…**), choose **Rename**, enter the new name and choose **Save name**. Only the name changes: the account, access and imported records stay as they are. The change appears in the connection's history.

## Change or reconnect a connection

**Connection settings** updates a connection’s access. For Shopify, **Continue to Shopify** authorizes the same store again, which also requests access that was added since. For JTL, enter the new access token, and the challenge code for on-premise; for Cin7 Core, the new application key. Then choose **Confirm replacement** and validate again. The account or address a connection points to can’t be changed; add a new connection for a different one.

When a connection shows **Access lost**, Luma also opens an issue. Choose **Reconnect** on the connection, or the reconnect button on the issue, and enter new access. The connection keeps its records and links, and sync resumes. See [Data sync](/docs/data-sync#when-a-sync-needs-you).

## Disconnect a connection

Open the connection’s actions menu (**…**), choose **Disconnect**, then **Confirm disconnect**. Luma deletes the stored credentials and stops new reads and changes. Requests already sent may still finish. Records the connection imported stay in Luma.

Disconnecting doesn’t revoke the access token in Shopify or JTL. To remove Luma’s access there too, uninstall the Luma app from the Shopify store, or remove Luma’s app registration or token in JTL.

## Uninstall an app

At the end of the app’s page, choose **Uninstall app**, then **Uninstall and disconnect**. Uninstalling:

- Disconnects every connection of the app and deletes their stored credentials.
- Removes the app’s permissions from all roles. Reinstalling doesn’t restore them; the dialog names the roles you’ll need to grant them to again.
- Keeps the records the app imported and its history. The app then appears under **Previously used apps**.

Requests already sent to your systems may still finish. As with disconnecting, revoke Luma’s access in Shopify or JTL as well.

## Observe-only

New workspaces start in observe-only. Luma can read your systems, detect issues, investigate
them and show proposed changes without writing to a provider. Proposals say **Would run when
writes are enabled**; you cannot approve them while observe-only applies.

Workspace owners can enable provider writes in **Settings → Workspace → Provider writes**.
In a connection's actions menu (**…**) on the app's page, owners can choose **Make
observe-only** for that connection, or **Use workspace write mode** to restore inheritance.
The connection's **Changes** fact shows which applies. A connection cannot enable writes
while the workspace observes only. Each mode change is recorded in the audit history.
Enabling writes keeps normal permissions, approvals and verification requirements. Work
already recorded as not executed needs a fresh proposal before it can run.

## Connect Starshipit

When Starshipit is available in your workspace, install the app and choose **Add connection**.
Enter a recognizable connection name and account name, then the **API key** and
**Subscription key** from Starshipit **Settings → API**. Choose **Save connection** and
**Validate access**. The account name is your label for the account connected by those keys.

After saving, Luma shows a change notification URL and an Authorization header once. Copy
both into Starshipit now; you can't view the secret again. Starshipit sends them with each
change notification, and Luma reads the current shipping state in response. If you lose
the secret, open **Connection settings** and choose **Generate a new webhook secret**. The
previous secret stops working immediately, so update Starshipit right away. Saving new keys
also replaces the secret. Each new secret is recorded in Activity.

Luma reads shipping orders and carrier tracking. Link its source records to your existing
orders before using them in an order change plan. Printed labels do not count as carrier
handoff. Corrections require separately enabled and confirmed write access; requesting
unconfirmed access makes the connection need attention.
