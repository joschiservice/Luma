title: Understand workflows
description: See what each workflow does, whether it can run here, what it covers and what to do next.
---

A workflow reacts to something — a person starting it, an event, a condition such as low stock, or a detected issue — and runs a fixed list of steps. Every action it takes passes workspace policy and approval before it runs, and a separate check confirms the result.

## Read the library

Each workflow shows two separate labels:

- **Enabled**, **Paused** or **Not set up** says whether someone turned it on. Paused means every activation or stock rule was revoked.
- **Ready**, **Partly blocked** or **Blocked** says whether it can run now. An enabled workflow can still be blocked, for example when a connection lost access or stock stopped updating.

Below the purpose, **Next** names the one thing to do, with a link to the place that fixes it. Use the views to find what needs you:

- **Needs attention** shows blocked or partly blocked workflows, workflows with decisions waiting and workflows whose latest run ended unresolved.
- **Enabled** and **Not enabled** filter by whether a workflow is turned on.

**Drafts** are workflows being written. Drafts never run.

## Open a workflow

A workflow has four parts:

- **Overview** shows when it starts, what success means and its readiness: the next action, anything blocking it, what it covers, who approves its actions, what waits for a person and what recent runs verified. **How it works** lists the steps in plain language.
- **Activity** shows every run with its decisions, actions and result checks. An accepted action isn't success on its own; only a passed result check confirms it.
- **Rules** is where you turn the workflow on, if your role allows it: stock rules for replenishment (**Manage procurement**), an activation that names the exact orders a correction may change (**Activate playbooks**), or **Turn on** for a workflow that starts from detected issues (**Publish and enable workflows**).
- **Technical details** keeps capability identifiers, permissions, inputs, version history and digests for inspection.

Coverage that Luma can't establish shows as **Unknown**, never as zero.

## Approval

**Approval** in the overview states how approval actually applies under current workspace policy. A replenishment rule's high-risk quantity only marks larger drafts as high risk. It never lets a draft skip approval.

## Set up replenishment

Open **Replenish low stock** and choose **Add stock rules**, or start from **Procurement** or a stock position. You can set up to 25 stock positions at once:

1. Choose the supplier and the stock positions. Search by SKU or use the page controls. Your selections stay selected across pages and searches. Positions that already have an active rule can't be selected.
2. Review each position. Luma shows current stock, whether the first check would propose an order, and who approves it. You can adjust the minimum and target for each position.
3. Activate. Each position gets its own rule and its own result. A rule that couldn't be confirmed can be retried; retrying never creates a second rule.

A stock position's page shows the same readiness as the workflow, including a waiting decision and the latest purchase order.

## Workflows that start from issues

Some workflows start on their own when Luma detects an issue:

- **Correct Shopify stock from the ERP** starts when Shopify and your ERP disagree on linked stock. It proposes setting Shopify to the ERP quantity and confirms it with a fresh Shopify read. It never changes ERP stock.
- **Release an overdue fulfillment hold** starts when a Shopify order stays on a fraud-review or manual hold for too long. It proposes releasing that hold and confirms in Shopify that it's gone. Payment, address and stock holds go to a person, because their cause has to be fixed first.
- **Check a failing connection** starts when a connection's reads keep failing or a provider stopped accepting Luma. Lost access goes to a person straight away, with **Reconnect** on the issue. Otherwise it waits 15 minutes, checks again, and hands the issue over only if reads still fail. It changes nothing.

Someone whose role includes **Publish and enable workflows** turns each one on under **Rules**. Runs act as that person, but every change still waits in [Needs you](/docs/needs-you) for someone else who can approve changes to approve. A proposal stays open for four hours. Right before acting, Luma reads stock again: if the change is still the same, it runs; if it would differ, Luma prepares one new proposal in the same Needs you item. A rejected change, a change Luma can't make safely, or an unclear result hands the issue to a person; nothing is retried behind your back.

## Workflows that follow up

Others start when something stays undone too long. They never change anything in your store or with your suppliers:

- **Follow up on an overdue supplier order** starts when a supplier hasn't confirmed a sent order in time, or a delivery is past its expected date. Luma notes the follow-up on the order, so nobody is asked yet, waits, and checks again. If the supplier is still silent, the order comes back to a buyer in Needs you.
- **Chase a refund after a return arrives** starts when returned items arrived but no refund is recorded within the waiting window. It hands the return to a person in Needs you. Luma never refunds by itself.
- **Follow up on a payment that stays pending** starts when an order's payment is still pending well after it was placed. It hands the order to a person in Needs you. Luma never holds or cancels it.

A follow-up leaves Needs you on its own once Luma sees it's done, for example when the refund is recorded or the payment settles. If a workflow can't run in your workspace, its template says what's missing, such as returns access or a supplier linked to your purchasing system, and links to where to fix it.

A workflow never closes an issue. Luma closes it only after its own check confirms the problem is gone. Releasing a hold, for example, doesn't ship the order, so the issue stays open until the warehouse makes progress.

Turning a workflow off stops new runs. A change that's waiting for approval is then never sent.
