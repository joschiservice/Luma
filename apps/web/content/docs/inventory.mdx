---
title: Compare stock across systems
description: See stock by SKU, location and source, find where your systems disagree, link the same SKU across systems and correct Shopify stock from your ERP.
---

Inventory shows the stock each connected store and ERP reports, by SKU, location and source. Use it to find where systems disagree, see what depends on a SKU’s stock and, with Shopify, correct the available quantity from your ERP. Open **Inventory** in the sidebar or press **G**, then **Y**.

## Find stock

Each row is one SKU at one location in one unit, with every source’s quantities stacked in the row. Choose a view:

- **All stock** shows every stock position.
- **Source differences** shows positions where sources report different quantities.
- **Incomplete stock** shows positions where a source hasn’t reported every quantity.

The view menu also lists the views you and your team saved, and opening **Inventory** from the sidebar returns to the view you used last.

Search by SKU or location. **Location** and **Source** above the list narrow it in one click; a source filter selects positions that source reports and still shows the other sources. For anything else, choose **Filter** and pick a field, such as **On hand**, **Available**, **Reserved**, **Incoming**, **Below safety stock**, **Differs in**, **Awaiting sync** or **Last read**. Quantity conditions let you choose a **Source**: **Any source**, **Every source**, one named source, or **Lowest across sources**, which compares the most cautious figure any source reports. A source that reports no quantity never counts as zero. Sort by SKU, location, a quantity (its lowest reported value) or the last read, in either direction. Links from a product or stock position show one SKU's positions; remove the **SKU** filter to see all.

Choose **Display** to switch between **Table** and **List**, choose columns, and group by location, SKU or source. Grouping by SKU shows one product across every warehouse. **Open records in a preview** shows a position's quantities by source, its orders and purchase orders beside the list. Changes mark the view **Modified**; choose **Save as new view** to keep it, see [Saved views](/docs/saved-views). **J** and **K** move through the positions, **Enter** opens one, **Space** previews it, **/** goes to search, **F** adds a filter and **?** lists every shortcut.

**No stock imported yet** means no connected system has shared stock; check [Data sync](/docs/data-sync) for progress. **No stock matches** means the search or filters exclude every position.

## Read the quantities

- **On hand**: the units the source reports at the location.
- **Committed**: Shopify units committed to orders or other supported operations.
- **Reserved**: the source’s separate reservation state. Shopify reserved stock is different from committed stock and isn’t compared with ERP order reservations.
- **Available**: the units the source can still sell. Below it, **Reported** means the source reported the quantity, and **Derived** means Luma calculated it as on hand minus reserved.
- **Incoming**: units the source reports as on their way. Purchase orders don’t count as incoming stock.

Quantities stay with their source and are never added across sources. If Shopify and your ERP both report the same warehouse, adding them would count the same units twice. Luma doesn’t treat any source as the authority, either: when quantities differ, compare the read times before you decide which one to trust.

**Not reported** means the source didn’t send that value. It isn’t zero: a reported 0 means the source says there’s no stock, while Not reported means Luma doesn’t know. **Removed at source** means the source no longer reports the position. Luma keeps its readings as evidence but leaves it out of comparisons.

## Read a stock position

Open a row to see one SKU at one location:

- The comparison at the top says whether **Reported quantities agree** or which quantities differ. Each source shows its SKU and location codes, its quantities, its **Availability method** and its **Freshness**: when Luma read it and when the source last updated it.
- **Replenishment** shows whether a stock rule reorders this SKU at this location. See [Workflows](/docs/workflows).
- **Orders with this SKU** lists order quantities as each source reports them. Luma can’t confirm which location fulfills them.
- **Supply commitments** lists purchase orders for the SKU. They aren’t confirmed incoming stock: Luma doesn’t know how much has arrived or at which location.
- **Stock issues** lists issues recorded for the SKU or this position.
- **SKU and locations** links to the product, the SKU record, all locations for this SKU and other SKUs at this location.
- **Linked sources** lists the source records Luma treats as this SKU.
- **Recent readings** lists the readings Luma recorded from each source, newest first. **Current** marks the reading Luma uses for that source. A reading shows what the source reported, not necessarily a stock movement.

Open **Unavailable stock and activation** to see damaged stock, quality control and safety stock. Location activity, item activation at that location and inventory tracking appear separately. A stored quantity does not prove the location can sell it. Missing facts stay **Not reported**. Shopify’s available quantity is reported directly, even when other quantities are unknown.

**Refresh stock** at the top of the page shows Luma’s latest readings. It doesn’t read the sources again; Luma reads them on its regular sync.

After a confirmed Luma correction, the source and recent readings show the quantity Luma set and whether that reading matches. Other people or apps may also have changed stock. A difference remains visible; a missing adjustment reference leaves the cause unknown.

## When stock is still syncing

A sale changes stock in one system first, and the other system catches up on its next sync. While that happens, the position shows **Syncing** instead of **Differs by source**. Open it to see when Luma first saw the gap and when it opens an issue if the gap is still there. Luma waits up to 30 minutes for your systems to agree again.

Luma opens an issue without waiting when a store shows stock that your stock source can't cover: the stock source has none left, or the store shows more than 25% above the stock source's available quantity. Choose the stock source in [Source authority](/docs/workspace-settings#source-authority).

A gap that is still open after the wait becomes a **Stock mismatch** issue that says how long Luma waited. An order shows its ordered items that are syncing in its **Issues** panel.

## When the stock sync stalls

The app or integration that copies stock from your stock source to your store can stop working. Then your store keeps selling against figures that get older with every sale, return or delivery.

Luma opens one **Stock sync stalled** issue for the store when, 15 minutes after your stock source changed, at least 3 linked products still show the old figure in the store, and at least half of the recent changes haven't arrived. You can change these limits under **Issue detection** in [Workspace settings](/docs/workspace-settings). Stock that Luma corrected itself doesn't count as a change the sync delivered. Before opening it, Luma reads those products from both systems again. Luma doesn't open the issue while its own reads of either system are failing or behind. The issue is high severity when the store shows more stock than your stock source has.

While the issue is open, products that differ in that store wait for the sync instead of opening their own **Stock mismatch** issue. A product that shows more stock in the store than your stock source has still gets its own issue, because it can oversell. Ask whoever runs your stock sync to check and restart it first: a product you correct earlier can be overwritten when the sync runs again. The issue closes when fresh reads show the products that lagged match again.

## Link the same SKU across systems

Luma treats records from different systems as the same SKU only after they’re linked, by a person or by an [automatic linking](/docs/data-sync#automatic-linking) rule. Until then, Shopify’s and your ERP’s stock for one item appear as separate SKUs, and you can’t compare them or correct one from the other.

1. Open the stock position and go to **Linked sources**. **Possibly the same SKU** lists SKUs in other systems with the same code.
2. Choose **Link** beside the match.
3. Check the reason under **Why is this correct?** and change it if needed, then choose **Link records**.

Luma then groups stock, products and order lines from both systems under one SKU, and each system keeps its own quantities. While it regroups them, the panel shows its progress. To separate a system again, choose **Unlink** beside it, then **Unlink source**. Every link and unlink is recorded with who made it and why.

Linking and unlinking need **Manage imports**. Without it, you see the linked sources and possible matches but not **Link** or **Unlink**.

## Correct Shopify stock

When Shopify’s available stock differs from your ERP’s, you can set Shopify to the quantity your ERP reports. Luma changes only available stock in Shopify, and never changes stock in your ERP. This needs **Correct Shopify inventory**. Without it, the stock position doesn’t show **Correct Shopify stock**.

Before you start, the SKU and location must be linked in both systems, both connections must be active, and Shopify must allow Luma to change inventory. Luma uses only ERP stock read in the last five minutes, and reads the ERP again when its data is older.

1. Open the stock position in Inventory. In **Correct Shopify stock**, choose the **Shopify store** and the **ERP source**.
2. Check **Available stock in each system**. The ERP is marked **Source of truth** and Shopify **To correct**, each with its read time. **Exact target and evidence** shows the Shopify inventory item and location, and the ERP reading the correction uses.
3. Choose **Review correction**. Luma shows the change, for example 12 → 8 available, and when the review expires.
4. Someone else whose role includes **Correct Shopify inventory** and **Approve or reject changes** opens the same stock position and chooses **Approve correction** or **Reject**. You can’t approve your own correction.
5. After approval, choose **Check approval** to update the review, then **Send to Shopify**. Only the person who requested the correction can send it.

Corrections waiting for approval, approved corrections waiting to be sent and results waiting for a check also appear in [Needs you](/docs/needs-you).

Luma reads both systems again before it sends. If stock changed in either system, or the review expired, it shows **Correction blocked**; choose **Review a new correction** to start again.

If Luma can’t offer a correction, it says why:

- **Reconnect Shopify and allow Luma to change inventory.** Someone with **Manage connections** reconnects the store in [Apps](/docs/apps-and-connections#change-or-reconnect-a-connection).
- **Stock data is more than five minutes old.** Choose **Refresh stock** in **Correct Shopify stock** so Luma reads both systems again. If the message stays, check the ERP’s sync in [Data sync](/docs/data-sync).
- **Shopify already shows the same quantity** as the ERP. No correction is needed.
- Shopify didn’t confirm that it tracks stock at this location.

## Check that the stock changed

A correction has three separate steps, and each shows its own state:

- **Review and approval**: the correction is waiting for approval, or approved.
- **Shopify accepted**: Shopify accepted the request. The quantity isn’t confirmed yet.
- **Quantity verified**: a fresh read matches the approved ERP quantity and confirms the adjustment reference for that correction.

Choose **Verify stock** to read Shopify again. Until the quantity is verified, don’t treat the stock as fixed. Stock issues for the position update after Luma’s next sync.

**Outcome unclear** means Luma couldn’t tell whether Shopify received the correction, for example because the connection was interrupted. Luma never sends the same correction twice. Check the stock in Shopify and choose **Verify stock** before you review a new correction.

If Luma cannot confirm the adjustment reference, the action stays uncertain even when the quantity matches. A matching quantity may be another change. Check Shopify before taking another action.

## Follow transfers and receiving

Choose **Transfers** in Inventory to follow stock moving between locations. Switch between
transfer requests and shipments in the view menu. Filter by source, requested, shipped, received
or unreceived quantity, request date or last source update, and sort by name or update; without
a sort, the newest requests come first. Open a record to inspect its exact source lines, requested,
ready, shipped and receiving quantities. These quantities provide context; Luma does not add
them to the stock reported at a location.

Transfer and shipment reads need separate Shopify grants. Enable the reads on the connection
and sync their record types. A location snapshot shows the historical name and address.
If the live location is unavailable, its name alone cannot link it to a current stock pool.

Accepted, rejected and unreceived quantities remain separate. Shopify's receipt total includes
cancelled units. A late arrival estimate means receipt is still unconfirmed; it does not prove
goods were lost. Luma pauses these checks when a source is unavailable, evidence is older than
five minutes or a line collection is unavailable.

Linked shipments and purchase orders appear only when an explicit relationship is recorded.
Open a linked purchase order for its receipt context. Procurement access is required.

Choose **Download receiving evidence** to retain the source identities, observed facts and
explicit links for investigation. Gather supplier confirmations, delivery documents and photos
separately, and confirm the claim deadline with the applicable provider policy. The download
does not submit a claim or change stock.

Open a bundle’s product to inspect **Bundle components** and the current source recipe. Each component links to its exact stock SKU when known. The recipe history lets you compare changes. Stock and fulfillment use the components; the merchandising parent does not add another physical balance. If components are unreadable, Luma shows unknown visibility. Shopify stock corrections direct you to component stock for known bundle parents.

## Review native stock

Open a stock position to see **Native stock** beside its source quantities. A position without
a reviewed opening has no native stock balance. Select a source, choose **Read stock source independently**, and enter the physical
opening quantities, including held or damaged units where applicable. Give a reason and choose
**Prepare opening review**. Another member with stock approval access inspects the exact proposed
movements and approves or rejects them. The preparer applies the approved proposal; changed
stock or source evidence requires a new review.

Location ownership advances through Observe, Shadow, Propose and Own with separate reviews.
Compare fresh source evidence before each step. Ownership does not publish Shopify quantities
or activate items. Existing provider commitments must be cleared before
native ownership. Handback requires aligned independent stock evidence and no remaining native
holds or uncertain effects.

For a Luma order, use **Native order reservations** to choose the exact order line and stock
location. Read shipments independently, then reserve stock. A shortage stays unallocated demand.
Read shipments again and choose **Consume verified shipped units** after units have shipped.
Preparing fulfillment does not consume stock. Release only the remaining hold. Order reductions
and cancellation release remaining units together with the order change; new shipments must be
consumed first. **Release due holds** checks this order's deadlines and retains holds when the
shipment evidence is stale or uncertain.

## Publish a native allocation

After your team has reviewed stock openings and moved this location through Shadow and
Propose to Own, open the stock position and choose **Publish native allocation**. Enter a
whole-unit allocation no larger than the native available balance. The allocation may be
smaller than the balance. Luma does not round fractional native stock for Shopify.

Choose **Review allocation**. Review the exact change, then have another authorized team
member approve it when policy requires independent approval. The requester can then choose
**Publish allocation**. If stock, mappings, authority or eligibility changes before dispatch,
review a new allocation. **Shopify accepted** means the write was accepted; choose **Verify
stock** for an independent quantity and eligibility check.

Stored quantity is separate from inventory item and location activation. Luma shows these
states independently and does not activate them during publication. A later native stock
change needs a new review and publication. When an outcome is uncertain, verify or
investigate it; Luma does not resend the same mutation.

Shopify’s published allocation is channel evidence. Its derived on-hand quantity does not
prove physical warehouse stock. Use an independent warehouse source for physical drift
and ledger changes. To hand stock authority back, clear holds and pending effects, restore
and verify the full native baseline, and obtain a separate ownership review. Activation and
other stock states need their own authorized actions.
